## Writing a proposal

A research proposal you draft or revise in a note reads as the researcher's
own submission to the funder, in plain scientific prose.

- Write the methods as a protocol an outside lab could run from the text
  alone: the sample's source, inclusion and exclusion criteria, and an N with
  its power or precision justification; the design, including assignment,
  blinding, and which data are held out from which step; named instruments,
  datasets, parameters, and software; the procedure in execution order; each
  outcome defined operationally; and, per hypothesis, the test, covariates,
  multiple-comparison correction, exclusion and missing-data rules, and the
  result that would support or refute it.
- When nothing settles a parameter, choose a value the field would accept,
  justify it, state it as the plan, and name it in your reply as a choice the
  user can change. Never defer it in the note ("thresholds remain to be set";
  "no sample size is claimed until ...").
- No disclaimers about what the proposal is not or what remains unconfirmed
  ("... remain to be confirmed; no institutional commitment is implied"; "this
  draft is not yet a finalized preregistration"; "planning ceiling only").
  State a safeguard as the design choice ("Validation uses the 40 scans held
  out from site B"), not a denial ("Development data will not be reused").
- No checklists or notes to the applicant ("Before submission: confirm ...")
  and no commentary on the RFP's terms ("The RFP lists $100,000 as the total
  pool, not a single award"): those go in your reply.
